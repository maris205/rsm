# Figure captions

**Figure 1. Local nonlinear lattice and its continuum description.** The upper panels compare the Störmer–Verlet trajectory at $N=64$ with the independently evolved Fourier–Galerkin continuum field, sampled on the same spatial grid. Both use $L=2\pi$, $v=\omega_0=g=1$, $\phi(x,0)=0.5\cos x$ and zero initial velocity. The lower panels show the fundamental and third spatial cosine coefficients; the third harmonic is generated by the cubic force. The corrected-field curve includes the leading $a^2\phi_{xxxx}/12$ term with a numerical Fourier cutoff $|k|\le24$. Parameters, phase and amplitude are not fitted. Axes are in dimensionless model units. These are synthetic trajectories, not observations.

**Figure 2. Separate spatial and temporal convergence, and the long-wave boundary.** Upper left: equal-weight global relative field RMS errors between accurate lattice ODE references and the ordinary or corrected continuum model. Halving the lattice spacing produces approximately second- and fourth-order convergence, respectively, for the stated smooth initial condition and finite time window. This panel does not claim fourth-order accuracy for the Verlet method. Upper right: Verlet time-step refinement at fixed $N=128$ against the same-lattice reference. Lower left: analytic continuous-time frequency errors over the $N=64$ lattice Brillouin zone; the dotted $ka=0.5$ line is a scale marker, not a proved universal cutoff. Near-grid-scale modes fall outside the accurate long-wave regime. Lower right: sampled relative deviations of the original lattice Hamiltonian under Verlet time integration. Symplecticity does not imply exact conservation of that Hamiltonian. A separate finest-grid precision audit retains one failed 1% reference-budget diagnostic; the measured smallest spatial-error metric changes by only 0.00781% on tightening its reference tolerance.
